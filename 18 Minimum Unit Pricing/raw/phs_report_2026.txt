Alcohol Consumption & Harms
Dashboard: Update Report
This report supports the latest update to the Public Health Scotland (PHS) Alcohol
Consumption and Harms Dashboard published on 30th June 2026.


The report presents key trends in estimated alcohol retail sales and wholly alcohol
attributable health harms and provides interpretation of some of the wholly and partially
alcohol attributable health harms data presented on the dashboard.

Data presented in this report are also available on the dashboard. The dashboard presents
data and evidence around key trends in alcohol consumption and alcohol-related health
and social harms, updated as data become available. Local reports showing indicators by
health board or local authority can be downloaded from the dashboard, where data are
available.




Preventing alcohol harm

Alcohol is a leading cause of poor health and early death in Scotland, and there are
significant inequalities in this harm. However, this is preventable. The data presented in
this report underline the urgency for a preventative and evidence-led approach to reducing
alcohol harm in Scotland. Public Health Scotland's Policy briefing: alcohol outlines key
policy proposals that could be enacted to prevent harm from alcohol in Scotland. For
further information about alcohol and public health, visit Public Health Scotland’s web
content on alcohol.

Key points

Overall alcohol sales in Scotland have declined, but estimated consumption per adult
drinker remains above the recommended guidance. Alcohol harms remain relatively
high, and wide health inequalities remain.

   •   In 2024, 1,179 people in Scotland died from a cause wholly attributable to alcohol,
       an average of almost 23 people per week.

   •   In 2024/25, there were 27,126 wholly alcohol attributable hospital admissions in

   •   There are stark inequalities in wholly alcohol attributable deaths and hospital

                        Total alcohol retail sales

In 2025, an estimated total of 40.4 million litres of pure alcohol were sold in Scotland.


This is equivalent to an average of 8.6 litres (L) sold per adult in Scotland per year. This is
the lowest level in the time series, a decrease from 2024 (9.1 L per adult). It remains
higher than the amount sold per adult in England & Wales (7.8 L per adult).

Excluding the proportion of the population that does not drink alcohol, adult drinkers in
Scotland in 2025 were estimated to drink 20.7 units of alcohol per week (10.7 L of alcohol
per year). This is higher than the UK Chief Medical Officers' guidance which recommends
drinking less than 14 units per week to keep health risks from alcohol to a low leveli.

Between 2017 and 2025, volume of pure alcohol sold per adult declined by 18.9% in
Scotland and by 16.0% in England & Wales.

                                                                                                         3
Alcohol retail sales by on & off trade

In 2025, an estimated 30.7 million litres of alcohol were sold in off-trade premises in
Scotland, equivalent to 6.5 L sold per adult. This is a decrease from 2024 (32.1 million
litres, 6.9 L sold per adult). This is the lowest level in the time series but is higher than the
amount sold per adult in off-trade premises in England and Wales (5.7 L sold per adult).

In 2025, the amount of alcohol sold in on-trade premises, such as pubs, clubs, and
restaurants, was slightly lower than the amount sold in 2024 (9.7 million litres in 2025
compared to 10.1 million litres in 2024). This is equivalent to 2.1 L sold per adult in 2025,
which is the same as the amount sold per adult in on-trade premises in England & Wales.

The majority (76.0%) of alcohol sold in Scotland in 2025 was sold in off-trade premises,
such as supermarkets and other off-licences. While total off-trade sales have declined
since 2017, the proportion of alcohol sold in the off-trade has increased from 72.9% in
2017. In Scotland, on-trade sales have declined more quickly than off-trade sales;
between 2017 and 2025, the total estimated volume of alcohol sold declined by 24.6% in
the on-trade and by 11.2% in the off-trade.

£2.46 in the on-trade.

The average price per unit of alcohol in the off-trade in Scotland has increased from 72p in

Alcohol health harms

Alcohol health harms can be characterised as wholly or partially attributable to
alcohol consumption. Wholly and partially alcohol attributable figures are measured
in different ways and provide insight to the public health burden related to alcohol.


Wholly attributable harms are directly and entirely related to alcohol consumption, for
example, alcoholic liver disease. These are sometimes called ‘alcohol-specific’. Wholly
alcohol attributable harms are measured by counting the deaths and hospital admissions
that are caused by conditions directly and entirely related to alcohol. This provides a very
consistent and stable measurement of the harm related to alcohol consumption.

For deaths, this includes only deaths that have been registered with cause-of-death
information that is directly and entirely related to alcohol consumption. For hospital
admissions, only those that record a diagnosis wholly attributable to alcohol consumption
are included in the calculation of wholly alcohol attributable hospital admissions.

Partially attributable harms are those for which alcohol consumption is one of a range of
possible causative factors, for example breast cancer. Partially attributable harms are
estimates and are calculated using a range of data sources including population drinking
patterns, alcohol consumption levels, associated health risks, and frequency of related
health conditions. This provides an estimate of the wider public health burden related to
alcohol consumption. Further information on how partially attributable harms are calculated
can be found in the Notes on data sources and methods section.

Trends in partially and wholly alcohol attributable harms are not exactly the same. This is
because they tell us different things about the harms related to alcohol consumption.
Differences may be due to population demographics, the time it takes for different health
conditions to lead to a death or hospital admission (the latency period), and prevalence of
other conditions where alcohol is a contributory factor.

The Alcohol Harms and Consumption Dashboard presents alcohol health harms by
Scottish Index of Multiple Deprivation (SIMD), including age, sex, and geographical area.
Data on health conditions is available for wholly and partially attributable hospital
admissions, and wholly attributable deaths.

                                      Wholly alcohol attributable deaths

In 2024, 1,179 people resident in Scotland died from a cause wholly attributable to
alcohol, which equates to an average of almost 23 people per week.


This is a decrease from 1,272 wholly attributable deaths in 2023 and is the same as the
rate of deaths as in 2016 (20.8 wholly alcohol attributable deaths per 100,000 population).

In 2024, there remained large inequalities in wholly alcohol attributable death rates:

                              •       The wholly alcohol attributable death rate in the 10% most deprived areas was
                                      almost six times higher than in the 10% least deprived areas (44.7 and 7.7 deaths
                                      per 100,000 population, respectively)

                              •       The wholly alcohol attributable death rate was more than twice as high amongst
                                      males than females (29.5 compared with 13.1, per 100,000 population)

                              •       The 10-year age group with the highest wholly alcohol attributable death rate was
                                      55 to 64 years (48.1 per 100,000 population). For males, the death rate in this age

Partially alcohol attributable deaths

Both the wholly and partially alcohol attributable death rates increased between 2012 and
2023. Between 2012 and 2018, the rate of partially alcohol attributable deaths followed a
similar pattern to the rate of wholly alcohol attributable deaths.

In 2019, the partially alcohol attributable death rate increased to a peak of 41.4 deaths per
100,000 population before decreasing to 34.3 deaths per 100,000 population in 2022. The
death rate then rose slightly to 35.7 deaths per 100,000 population in 2023.

This is different to the rate of wholly alcohol attributable deaths, which decreased in 2019
to 18.4 deaths per 100,000 population before rising to 22.4 deaths per 100,000 population
in 2023.

In 2024, the wholly alcohol attributable death rate fell to 20.8 deaths per 100,000
population. Currently, partially attributable death figures are available up to 2023; the rate
of partially alcohol attributable deaths for 2024 will be published on the dashboard later in
the year (see Notes on methods and data sources).

        Wholly alcohol attributable hospital admissions

In 2024/25, there were 27,126 wholly attributable hospital inpatient stays in general
acute hospitals; this is equivalent to an age-sex standardised rate of 493.6 stays per
100,000 population.


In total, 17,955 people in Scotland were admitted to a general acute hospital with a wholly
attributable diagnosis in 2024/25, meaning that some people were admitted more than
once.

In 2024/25, there remain large inequalities in wholly attributable hospital admissions:

   •    The rate of wholly attributable hospital admissions in the 10% most deprived areas
        in Scotland was over seven times higher than in the 10% least deprived areas
        (1,237.5 per 100,000 population compared with 170.0 per 100,000 population).

   •    The wholly attributable hospital admissions rate was more than twice as high
        amongst males than females (698.9 compared with 288.2 per 100,000 population)

   •    Overall and for males, the 10-year age group with the highest wholly attributable
        hospital admissions rate was 55 to 64 years (821.8 per 100,000 population overall,
        1,156.4 per 100,000 population for males). For females, the 10-year age group with
        the highest wholly attributable hospital admissions rate was 45-54 years (497.2 per
        100,000 population).

In 2024/25, the leading conditions for wholly alcohol attributable hospital stays in general
acute hospitals were:

   •    Withdrawal state, 140.1 per 100,000 population

   •    Harmful use, 131.4 per 100,000 population

   •    Acute intoxication, 99.0 per 100,000 population

   •    Alcoholic dependence syndrome, 73.2 per 100,000 population

   •    Cirrhosis, 59.4 per 100,000 population.

Partially alcohol attributable hospital admissions

Rates of partially alcohol attributable hospital admissions have followed a similar trend to
rates of wholly alcohol attributable hospital admissions and have declined since 2012.

However, the decline in rates of partially attributable hospital admissions has been more
gradual; the rate of wholly alcohol attributable hospital admissions declined by 22%
between 2012/13 and 2023/24 while the rate of partially alcohol attributable hospital
admissions declined by just over 5% between 2012 and 2023. In 2023, the partially alcohol
attributable hospital admission rate was 421.1 per 100,000 population.

In 2024/25, the wholly alcohol attributable hospital admissions rate fell to 493.6 admissions
per 100,000 population. Currently, partially attributable hospital admissions figures are
available up to 2023; the rate of partially alcohol attributable hospital admissions for 2024
will be published on the dashboard later in the year.

Notes on data sources and methods

Detailed information on data sources and methods for calculating the figures presented in
this report can be found on the PHS Alcohol Consumption and Harms Dashboard in
the Data Sources & Methods tab.



Alcohol retail sales

Please note that the alcohol retail sales figures presented are estimates.

Public Health Scotland uses off-trade alcohol retail sales data from Circana to produce per
adult sales estimates contained in this briefing and the Alcohol Consumption and Harms
Dashboard; copyright for off-trade data remains with Circana. Please note that the off-
trade alcohol sales data presented in this report and in the dashboard are not comparable
with data published in previous MESAS Monitoring Reports, as those data were obtained
from a different data provider. For a comparison of different off-trade data sources for
estimating alcohol retail sales, please see this report: Estimating population alcohol
consumption in Scotland: the impact of using different sources of alcohol retail
sales data.

Public Health Scotland obtains on-trade alcohol retail sales data from Nielsen IQ to
produce per adult sales estimates contained in this briefing and the Alcohol Consumption
and Harms Dashboard; copyright for these data remains with Nielsen IQ. On-trade data
used in this report/dashboard were produced in 2026, based on OPM data - 2025 Period
13.

Retail sales estimates may differ slightly to those previously published as they continue to
be improved retrospectively after being supplied. Consequently, the most recent data
provided are considered to be the best available because they provide the most robust
picture of the alcohol sales market.



Alcohol deaths

Please note that in this report, and on the PHS Alcohol Consumption and Harms
Dashboard, wholly alcohol attributable deaths are included if the deceased was resident

Partially attributable statistics

Public Health Scotland have adopted the International Model of Alcohol Harms and
Policies (InterMAHP) methodology for calculating Alcohol Attributable Fractions. Data
used as inputs for this include relative risk functions from the World Health Organisation
(WHO), prevalence data from the Scottish Health Survey, and annual per capita
consumption data estimated using alcohol retail sales data, outlined above. Partially
attributable harms data are updated on the dashboard later than wholly attributable harms
data as they are reliant on publication and processing of survey data.

The InterMAHP methodology includes conditions that are both wholly and partially
attributable to alcohol. The conditions in the InterMAHP methodology that are wholly
attributable to alcohol have not been included in the partially attributable statistics as these
are available elsewhere on the dashboard.

Further information on the methodology used for calculating wholly and partially
attributable statistics can be found on the dashboard.
