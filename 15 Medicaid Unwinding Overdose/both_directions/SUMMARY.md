**FLAG:** First-stage gate failed in the loss direction: in loss states, larger adult enrollment losses did not lower Medicaid buprenorphine relative to other states, so the loss-direction death results say nothing about the buprenorphine channel. Death analyses are underpowered (MDE 22–50% of the baseline rate), and the death event-study leads fail the pre-trend test (p = 0.03). Several deviations from the brief: no earlier "main unwinding run" existed, so the H1 anchor model was re-implemented; the synthetic-control scale and the SC power method were changed after the first run (proportional scale; MDE = 2.8 × SD of placebo gaps); log-buprenorphine and state-trend checks were added post hoc. NC's VSRR 2023 overdose count is 16% below CDC's own predicted value, a data anomaly right before expansion. Official state/CMS pages were blocked, so the expansion dates rest on peer-reviewed sources [REF NEEDED: primary source].

**Do gains and losses act symmetrically?** For buprenorphine they did not in point estimate: gaining coverage raised Medicaid buprenorphine, while losing it did not measurably lower it. ### 1. What the code did

1. Downloaded raw data. CMS PI enrollment (Oct 2026 release), CMS renewal outcomes and SDUD 2018–2026 came through the data.medicaid.gov datastore API, because download.medicaid.gov was blocked. Also downloaded CDC VSRR xkb8-kh2a (through the 12-month period ending Jun 2026), Census civilian population by single year of age (vintages 2020 and 2025), FRED state unemployment, and the FDA NDC directory (current + excluded) → `inputs/`, checksums in `inputs/CHECKSUMS.md`.
2. Built adult enrollment as total Medicaid/CHIP minus child enrollment, using Final Report = Y rows only. AZ uses total enrollment as a proxy, because it reports no child count for Jan 2022–Apr 2024. RI Dec 2024 was interpolated. Derived values match CMS's own adult field (which starts Jul 2024) exactly → `enroll_month`, 51 states, Jan 2022–Jun 2026.
3. Computed net % change in adult enrollment vs March 2023, set to 0 before Apr 2023. Quarterly and annual values are means of the monthly values. Kink at 0: `gain_pp` = max(change, 0), `loss_pp` = max(−change, 0). At 2024Q4 the change was NC +35.6%, SD +35.5%, and −36.3% to −5.5% in the other 49 states (median −23.4%) → `exposure_q`, `exposure_y`.
4. Took expansion status from the CMS PI flag. Status changed during 2018–2025 in 9 states (VA, ME, ID, UT, NE, OK, MO, NC, SD), and 10 states never expanded → `expansion_state`.
5. Summarised renewal outcomes by state (Mar 2023–Mar 2024): procedural share, ex parte share, first termination month. "Delayed" = first termination in Jun 2023 or later; "heavy ex parte" = top quartile → `renewal_state`.
6. Classified SDUD buprenorphine products: 480,578 rows pulled, of which 318,305 were not buprenorphine (mostly bupropion). Products were matched to the FDA NDC directory. Sublingual/buccal OUD products = 108,089 rows (including 9,186 unmatched generic rows with ≥10 units per Rx). Long-acting injectables = 9,094 rows, kept for a sensitivity only. Pain products = 45,090 rows, excluded → `sdud`.
7. Aggregated SDUD to state × quarter, summing fee-for-service and managed-care rows and dropping the national "XX" rows. Suppressed cells (37% of rows) were set to 5, with 0 as a sensitivity. The panel ends at 2025Q4, because the 2026Q1 national total was 86% of the prior quarter → `sdud_q`.
8. Built the deaths panel from 12-month-ending December VSRR values, 2015–2025: total, opioid, synthetic-opioid, psychostimulant and non-overdose deaths, plus CDC predicted overdose counts. NY = NY + NYC. Drug-specific values are missing for 52–55 state-years in 2018–2025 → `deaths_year`, `deaths_month`.
9. Built population (total, and adults 18+) and unemployment (quarterly/annual means) → `pop_year`, `unemp_q`, `unemp_y`.
10. Assembled the panels: bupe per 100,000 adults (51 states × 2018Q1–2025Q4 = 1,632 state-quarters, no missing values) and deaths per 100,000 (51 × 2015–2025). The state flags include the donor pool: no expansion change 2018–2025 and middle tertile of loss → 14 states: DE, GA, KS, KY, MA, MI, MN, ND, NJ, OH, PA, RI, VT, WA → `clean_data.xlsx` (5 sheets).
11. S0 cross-section: kinked `lm` of the change in bupe (mean 2024Q3–2025Q2 minus mean 2022Q2–2023Q1) and of the change in deaths (2025 minus 2022) on gain/loss at 2024Q4, n = 51 → `s0_model_bup_cs`, `s0_model_od_cs`.
12. S0 panel first stage: `feols(bup_per100k ~ gain_pp + loss_pp | state + qtr)`, clustered by state, n = 1,632 → `s0_model_first_stage`.
13. S0 continuous MDE: the same kinked model on 2018–2022 data, with each state's real 2023–24 exposure shifted 2 years (deaths, n = 255) or 8 quarters (bupe, n = 1,020) earlier. MDE = 2.8 × SE → `s0_mde_continuous`.
14. S0 SC MDE: placebo SCs for each of the 14 donors in a fake window (pre 2015–2020 / post 2021–22 for deaths; pre 2018Q1–2020Q4 / post 2021Q1–2022Q4 for bupe). MDE = 2.8 × SD of the placebo gaps → `s0_mde_sc`.
15. H1: loss states only (49 states), time-varying `loss_pp` and fixed intensity × post, for bupe (n = 1,568) and deaths 2018–2025 (n = 392) → `h1_model_*`.
16. H2 SC: proportional synthetic control for NC and SD on bupe (post from 2023Q4 for NC, 2023Q3 for SD) and deaths (post 2024–2025; SD's 2023 dropped as a transition year). Inference by in-space permutation over the 14 donors → `h2_table`, `h2_placebo`.
17. H2 robustness: placebo timing one year earlier; donor pool = the 10 never-expansion states; CDC predicted counts; NC with 2023 dropped from the pre-period; NC opioid-involved deaths (6 donors with complete data) → `h2_table_robust`, `h2_table_deaths_alt`, `h2_nc_opioid`.
18. H3 unified kinked models, clustered by state: bupe (n = 1,632) and deaths (n = 408). Symmetry Wald test of b_gain + b_loss = 0 (t, 50 df) and delta-method CI for b_loss/b_gain → `h3_model_bup`, `h3_model_od`, `h3_symmetry`.
19. H3 event studies with exposure fixed at 2024Q4: bupe 2021Q1–2025Q4 (ref 2023Q1) and deaths 2018–2025 (ref 2022), plus a joint Wald test of the leads → `h3_model_bup_es`, `h3_model_od_es`.
20. H3 robustness. For bupe, 11 variants: suppressed = 0, + LAI, + unemployment, prior expansion × quarter FE, procedural share × post, excluding delayed/heavy-ex-parte loss states (23 flagged; NC and SD kept), excluding AZ, weighted, fixed exposure, log, state trends. For deaths, 10 variants: the same where applicable + log, CDC predicted, state trends. Outcome variants: opioid, synthetic opioid, and two placebo outcomes (psychostimulant, non-overdose) → `h3_table_robust`.
21. Leave-one-state-out (51 refits per outcome) → `h3_loso`.
22. Saved results and figures → `results.rds`, `figures/` (8 PNGs, made in `figures_tables.Rmd`), `analysis.html`, `figures_tables.html`.

**Unverified:** SD 1 Jul 2023 and NC 1 Dec 2023 are confirmed only through peer-reviewed papers (PMIDs 40878023, 41027709, 41370080, 38733650); no statute or CMS approval was read. Whether the unmatched generic NDCs are all sublingual is inferred from units per Rx. NC's 2023 VSRR shortfall is not explained.
**Not run:** the NC opioid-settlement spending could not be measured. The NC pre-trend was checked through SC pre-fit, placebo timing and event studies only. The SD opioid-specific SC was not run (sparse data).

### 2. Methods and Results

**Methods.** We built a panel of the 50 states and DC. The exposure was the net percentage change in adult Medicaid/CHIP enrollment (total minus child enrollment; CMS Performance Indicator data) relative to March 2023, set to zero before April 2023. A piecewise-linear term with a kink at zero gave separate slopes per percentage point (pp) gained and per pp lost. Medicaid buprenorphine came from the State Drug Utilization Data, 2018Q1–2025Q4: sublingual and buccal buprenorphine and buprenorphine-naloxone products identified through FDA NDC directory linkage, fee-for-service and managed care combined, suppressed cells (1–10) imputed as 5. It was expressed as prescriptions per 100,000 adults. Overdose deaths were CDC VSRR provisional 12-month-ending December counts per 100,000 population, 2018–2025. For H3, we fitted two-way fixed-effects models (state and quarter or year) with standard errors clustered by state. Symmetry was tested as b_gain + b_loss = 0, and the ratio b_loss/b_gain had a delta-method 95% CI. For H2, we estimated synthetic controls for North Carolina (expansion 1 Dec 2023) and South Dakota (1 Jul 2023). Each state's series was scaled to its own pre-period mean. The donors were 14 states with no expansion change in 2018–2025 that fell in the middle tertile of enrollment loss. Inference used permutation over donors (minimum attainable p = 1/15). For H1, we fitted continuous-intensity models restricted to the 49 loss states. Minimum detectable effects (80% power, α = 0.05) came from placebo exposure in 2018–2022. Gain-side slopes are identified by two states (NC and SD), so their cluster-robust standard errors are unreliable. Arizona's exposure used total enrollment as a proxy. Analyses used R 4.3.3, fixest 0.14.2, quadprog 1.5.8 and tidyverse 2.0.0.

**Results.** Adult enrollment rose 35.6% in NC and 35.5% in SD by 2024Q4 and fell by a median of 23.4% elsewhere (range −36.3% to −5.5%). *First stage.* In the panel (n = 1,632 state-quarters), each pp gained raised Medicaid buprenorphine by 11.8 Rx per 100,000 adults per quarter (95% CI 1.1 to 22.5; p = 0.031). Each pp lost changed it by +1.8 (−13.3 to 16.8; p = 0.81). The cross-section (n = 51) gave similar estimates: gain 11.5 (−10.7 to 33.7; p = 0.30), loss 0.4 (−16.3 to 17.1; p = 0.96). In synthetic control, Medicaid buprenorphine was 43.4% above its synthetic counterpart in NC (+231 Rx per 100,000; ratio-rank p = 0.20; one-sided gap-rank p = 0.067) and 162% above in SD (+169; p = 0.067 for both). *Power.* The minimum detectable change in deaths was 0.50 per 100,000 per pp gained and 0.26 per pp lost; for a 30-pp change this is 44% and 22% of the 2022 mean rate. The SC MDE was 50% of the pre-period mean for both states. *H1.* Among loss states, each pp lost changed buprenorphine by +2.2 (−13.5 to 17.8; p = 0.78; n = 1,568) and overdose deaths by −0.087 per 100,000 (−0.36 to 0.19; p = 0.53; n = 392). The fixed-intensity versions gave +3.1 (p = 0.72) and −0.157 (p = 0.30). *H2.* After expansion, overdose deaths were 5.3% below synthetic in NC (−1.4 per 100,000; p = 0.93) and 43.5% above synthetic in SD (+3.8; p = 0.47). Using CDC predicted counts gave −10.8% for NC (p = 0.47) and +38.1% for SD (p = 0.53). Dropping NC's 2023 from the pre-period gave −14.3% (p = 0.40). NC opioid-involved deaths were 27.8% below synthetic (6 donors; no permutation test). In the placebo-timing checks, bupe gaps were +1.7% for NC and +41.2% for SD, and death gaps were −20.0% for NC and −28.1% for SD. With never-expansion donors, bupe gaps were +45.2% (NC) and +145.5% (SD). *H3.* In the unified model (n = 408 state-years), the death slope was 0.013 per pp gained (SE 0.129; p = 0.92) and −0.085 per pp lost (SE 0.134; p = 0.53). Symmetry was not rejected for buprenorphine: b_gain + b_loss = 13.5 (SE 12.3; p = 0.28), with a ratio of 0.15 (−1.02 to 1.32). It was not rejected for deaths either: sum −0.072 (SE 0.243; p = 0.77), ratio −6.7 (−162 to 149). Event-study leads were jointly non-zero for deaths (p = 0.032) but not for buprenorphine (p = 0.13), where gain-side leads trended upward. *Robustness.* The buprenorphine gain slope stayed positive in all 10 level-scale variants (3.6 to 12.9). It lost significance with prior-expansion × quarter FE (p = 0.56), with state trends (9.6; p = 0.13) and when delayed/ex parte states were excluded (p = 0.16). On the log scale it was +2.4% per pp gained (p = 0.050) versus +0.5% per pp lost (p = 0.54). No loss slope was significant (−8.2 to 3.1). All death specifications were null (gain −0.12 to 0.26, loss −0.18 to 0.12; all p ≥ 0.26), as were opioid, synthetic-opioid, psychostimulant (gain p = 0.10, loss p = 0.14) and non-overdose (gain p = 0.10, loss p = 0.44) deaths. Leave-one-state-out ranges were 9.0 to 14.2 for the bupe gain slope and −0.12 to −0.001 for the death loss slope.

### 3. Recap

- Combined unwinding losses (49 states) and the NC/SD expansion gains in one kinked dose-response model, with synthetic controls for NC and SD, using SDUD buprenorphine and VSRR deaths for 2018–2025.
- Gaining coverage raised Medicaid buprenorphine (NC +43%, SD +162%); losing coverage did not detectably lower it. Overdose deaths showed no detectable effect in either direction, and the death analyses are underpowered and show pre-trends.
- Decide whether to pursue this as a buprenorphine-only asymmetry paper (deaths reported as an underpowered null), and check NC's 2023 VSRR count against final NCHS data before relying on the NC death results.
