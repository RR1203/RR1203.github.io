About

Physician and psychiatric researcher. I study how childhood adversity shapes the way the brain and the body answer stress — with neuroimaging, magnetic resonance spectroscopy and machine learning.

MD thesis at Friedrich Schiller University Jena with Dr Lejla Colic and Prof Dr Martin Walter. Approbation — the full German licence to practise medicine — in 2025.

In the clinic

The measurements are worth taking because of what happens in the room. Psychiatry is the one part of medicine where the instrument is the conversation.

  • Clinical psychiatryThe work of sitting with someone long enough to understand what is actually happening to them, and of being useful while the picture is still unclear.
  • PsychotherapyWhere the evidence and the encounter meet. I want to be as careful with the hour as I am with the data.
  • Time with patientsThe part I would not trade. Everything else — the imaging, the models, the papers — is downstream of it.

Research & Publications

One question runs through all of it: why the same hardship leaves one person ill and another well. I work at the join between psychiatry and neuroscience, following that question from the acute stress response through the long course of depression, and I care most about findings that survive contact with a second sample.

01

Childhood adversity & the stress response

What early adversity leaves behind in the way body and brain answer an acute psychosocial stressor — cortisol, network dynamics, and what separates the people who recover quickly from those who do not.

02

The course of depression

Why an episode lifts, lingers or returns. Chronicity, remission and relapse traced through large longitudinal cohorts rather than a single time point.

03

Imaging, metabolites & computation

The methods that make the first two questions answerable: fMRI and MR spectroscopy alongside physiological signals, read with machine learning and judged by whether the result generalises.
