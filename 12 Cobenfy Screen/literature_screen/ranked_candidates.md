# Ranked candidates: Cobenfy (xanomeline-trospium) repurposing and predicted harms

Scores: v3 scoring, 0–14 scale. Each human type counts 2, animal counts 1 and only alongside a human type, and tiny or contradictory evidence is halved; full rules are in `decisions_log.md`. "Total papers" is the PubMed hit count for the condition, used as the literature-bias check. All citations are PMIDs retrieved in this run.
**Caveat that applies to every entry:** most human-pharmacology evidence comes from **cholinesterase inhibitors**, not M1/M4 agonists. It supports "more cholinergic tone helps" but does not prove the effect is M1/M4-specific.

---

## A. Top 10 untested indications (benefit)

### 1. Parkinson's disease psychosis | score 8.0 | 5 evidence types (4 human) | total papers 3,865
- **Antipsychotic response:** clozapine beat placebo in 16 RCTs (SMD −1.31). Quetiapine was inferior (PMID 38412585, meta-analysis, n=1,252).
- **Human pharmacology:** in an IPD meta-analysis, ChEIs reduced delusions and hallucinations in PD, but the effects were small (PMID 37358841). A follow-up analysis found only the PD-delusions effect survived correction (PMID 37530610).
- **Imaging:** PD patients with visual hallucinations had reduced cholinergic FEOBV-PET uptake in the left ventral visual stream (PMID 38864492, n=38 PD + 10 controls).
- **Animal:** xanomeline attenuated MK-801 hyperlocomotion and PPI deficits in mice, comparable to pimavanserin (PMID 42345506).
- **Clinical observation (Cobenfy):** in one 83-year-old with PD psychosis that had failed quetiapine, brexpiprazole and pimavanserin, symptoms resolved almost completely on 100/20 mg BID (PMID 42729706, n=1).
- **Mechanism:** M4 agonism lowers striatal dopamine release without D2 blockade, and M1 agonism offsets cortical cholinergic loss. This is the mechanism a D2-sensitive PD brain would need.
- **Trospium caveat:** none for efficacy, because the effect is central. Trospium may help, since it reduces peripheral cholinergic adverse effects in a frail elderly population.
- **Safety flag:** xanomeline induced dystonia in EPS-sensitised monkeys (PMID 12700711). The EMERGENT trials showed no EPS signal in schizophrenia (PMID 43010316, n=683). Motor effects in PD remain unknown.
- **Unmet need:** in the US only pimavanserin is approved. Clozapine requires blood monitoring, and quetiapine fails.
- **First test:** a records check (e.g., Mayo) of patients with parkinsonism who received Cobenfy off-label, recording psychosis course and UPDRS-III. Then a small open-label trial (n≈15–20, 6 wk) with NPI-hallucinations/SAPS-PD as the primary outcome and MDS-UPDRS-III as the safety outcome.

### 2. Tourette syndrome / tics | score 7.0 | 5 types (3 human) | total papers 8,582
- **Antipsychotic response:** in 39 RCTs, first-generation antipsychotics reduced tic severity by SMD −0.65 and second-generation by SMD −0.71 vs placebo (PMID 36528030, n=4,578).
- **Human pharmacology:** open-label donepezil reduced YGTSS tic score from 18.6 to 12.2 (P=0.006), but 50% of patients withdrew (PMID 18343255, n=20).
- **Postmortem:** cholinergic interneurons in the caudate and putamen were reduced by 50–60% (PMID 19941350, n=5 TS). This is contradicted by PMID 1972320 (n=4) and by VAChT-PET (PMID 28791334), both null.
- **Animal:** xanomeline and an M4 PAM reduced tic-like behaviour in two TS mouse models. The effect was blocked by M4 antagonists (PMID 38689378).
- **Clinical observation:** tics emerged or worsened after anticholinergic drugs (PMID 16869393, n=2).
- **Mechanism:** striatal M4 activation dampens D1-pathway output, the circuit thought to generate tics.
- **Trospium caveat:** none; the effect is central.
- **Unmet need:** approved antipsychotics (haloperidol, pimozide, aripiprazole) cause metabolic and EPS burden.
- **First test:** a small open-label trial in adults (n≈15, YGTSS, 8 wk). The animal data are M4-specific, which makes this the most mechanism-specific candidate.

### 3. Cognitive impairment after traumatic brain injury | score 7.0 | 4 types (3 human) | total papers 14,621
- **Human pharmacology:** in an RCT, donepezil beat placebo on verbal learning, with 42% vs 18% responders and NNT 3.5 (PMID 39628282, n=75). A meta-analysis found d=1.68 (PMID 38294196). In a crossover trial, oral physostigmine improved memory in 44% of patients (PMID 7804294, n=36).
- **Anticholinergic burden:** a rise in ACB during rehab predicted worse dependency (OR 9.65; PMID 26287759, n=52, mixed brain/spinal injury).
- **Imaging:** reduced basal-forebrain grey matter tracked attention and learning deficits (PMID 15548553, n=31).
- **Animal:** an M1 agonist improved water-maze performance after rat TBI (PMID 9259007).
- **Mechanism:** TBI damages basal-forebrain cholinergic projections, and a direct M1 agonist bypasses lost presynaptic ACh. ChEIs depend on remaining presynaptic ACh.
- **Trospium caveat:** none. Trospium should reduce the GI adverse effects that hurt donepezil (46% vs 8% AEs in PMID 39628282).
- **Unmet need:** no approved drug.
- **First test:** a records check for TBI patients on Cobenfy for psychosis or agitation after TBI (HVLT-R if available), then a pilot RCT copying the MEMRI-TBI-D design.

### 4. Dementia with Lewy bodies (cognition/behaviour) | score 6.0 | 3 human types | total papers 15,394
- **Human pharmacology:** pooled trials showed ChEIs give small cognitive, functional and global gains in DLB (PMID 25941104).
- **Anticholinergic burden:** strong anticholinergic exposure predicted dementia more strongly for DLB than for AD (PMID 41121438, n≈399,000).
- **Postmortem/imaging:** Lewy-body pathology contributes to basal-forebrain cholinergic degeneration (PMID 39865328, n=455 autopsies).
- **Antipsychotic response:** null. Antipsychotics were not effective in LBD psychosis (PMID 32662076, 19 RCTs; PMID 26085043). This points toward a non-D2 mechanism such as M1/M4.
- **Mechanism:** DLB has the deepest cortical cholinergic deficit of the dementias, and its psychosis is cholinergic rather than dopaminergic.
- **Trospium caveat:** none centrally. Autonomic failure in DLB raises the bradycardia/syncope risk (see harms).
- **Unmet need:** no FDA-approved DLB drug, and antipsychotic sensitivity is dangerous.
- **First test:** extend the existing AD-psychosis (ADEPT) protocol to DLB. A records check of Cobenfy use in DLB is fastest.

### 5. Down syndrome cognition / DS-associated AD | score 5.5 | 3 types (2.5 human) | total papers 2,336
- **Human pharmacology:** open-label rivastigmine helped (PMID 17201619, n=11), but an RCT was null (PMID 27061338, n=22). The type is therefore halved.
- **Anticholinergic burden:** anticholinergic users with DS had worse executive function (PMID 41078246, n=108).
- **Imaging/postmortem:** cholinergic basal-forebrain volume falls in the 40s and tracks cognition (PMID 41521149, n=358). FEOBV uptake is lower with amyloid (PMID 40189807, n=30). ChAT is reduced at autopsy (PMID 6228286).
- **Animal:** Ts65Dn mice show cholinergic neuron degeneration (PMID 15869949).
- **Mechanism:** early, predictable cholinergic degeneration.
- **Trospium caveat:** none.
- **Unmet need:** no approved drug.
- **First test:** the RCT evidence is weak, so do PET/biomarker-stratified pilot work first.

### 6. Parkinson's disease cognitive impairment | score 5.0 | 3 types (2 human) | total papers 33,890
- **Human pharmacology:** a subthreshold scopolamine dose impaired memory in PD but not in controls (PMID 3631918). The M1 PAM TAK-071 improved a cognitive composite in PD (PMID 39761063, n=54; primary gait endpoint negative).
- **Imaging/postmortem:** cortical cholinergic PET levels track attention and memory (PMID 38109781, n=101). CHRM1 protein is lost in PD hippocampus (PMID 36155524).
- **Contradicting evidence:** anticholinergic burden was unrelated to cognition (PMID 38797973, n=493).
- **Status:** untested for Cobenfy. The M1 PAM class has been tested (NCT04334317).
- **Unmet need:** rivastigmine gives only modest benefit.
- **First test:** combine with candidate #1 and add cognitive endpoints.

### 7. Alcohol use disorder | score 4.0 | 3 types (1.5 human) | total papers 94,877
- **Postmortem:** cortical muscarinic receptor density reduced by 30–40% (PMID 3067606, n=79; PMID 2541736). M4 is downregulated in the putamen, and an M4 PAM reduced alcohol self-administration (PMID 32331824).
- **Human pharmacology:** contradictory. Galantamine RCT was null (PMID 16328375, n=149), and an open-label donepezil study was positive (PMID 33284058, n=11).
- **Animal:** xanomeline + trospium reduced ethanol intake in 3 of 4 monkeys (PMID 42562046).
- **Status:** weak human evidence. Cocaine use disorder is already in trials (NCT07624227), which makes AUD the natural next substance target.

### 8. Huntington's disease | score 4.0 (also harm score 2.0, so evidence is mixed) | total papers 26,258
- **Postmortem:** reduced ChAT and muscarinic binding in caudate (PMID 4733, n=32).
- **Human pharmacology:** mostly null. Donepezil RCT null (PMID 17030764, n=30). A Cochrane review found one positive trial for verbal fluency (PMID 25734590).
- **Antipsychotic response:** antipsychotic use was linked to faster decline (PMID 42912861).
- **Status:** low priority. M4-mediated chorea control is plausible but has no direct evidence.

### 9. Post-stroke cognition | score 4.0 | 2 human types | total papers 31,851
- **Human pharmacology:** in a Cochrane NMA of vascular cognitive impairment, donepezil gave a small ADAS-Cog gain (PMID 33704781, n=4,373). In post-stroke aphasia, donepezil had SMD 0.8 (PMID 29984673).
- **Postmortem:** cholinergic deficit in small-vessel vascular dementia (PMID 21988073). Contradicted by PMID 19776575 (n=96).
- **Status:** effects are small, and the trials are not M1/M4-specific.

### 10. ADHD | score 3.5 | 3 types (2 human, all small) | total papers 53,577
- **Human pharmacology:** galantamine was null in adult ADHD (PMID 16633145, n=36), and donepezil was null in two small open studies.
- **Other evidence:** lymphocyte muscarinic binding was 55% lower in ADHD children (PMID 19863190). This is a peripheral proxy.
- **Status:** the weakest of the ten, included only to complete the top 10.

**Excluded despite scores ≥4:**
- **Sjögren's / xerostomia (5.0) and gastroparesis (2.0):** both peripheral M3 effects, which trospium blocks by design.
- **Epilepsy (benefit 3.5, harm 3.0):** contradictory evidence.
- **Schizophrenia cognition:** inside the approved population.
- **Autism irritability, AD psychosis, AD cognition, bipolar mania, cocaine:** already in trials.

## B. Top 5 predicted harms

| Rank | Condition | Harm score | Evidence chain | Trospium protects? | Suggested check |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Bradycardia / syncope / conduction disease** | 5.0 | ChEIs raise cardiovascular AEs, incl. syncope, 2–5-fold (PMID 25941104). In healthy volunteers there were 2 syncope cases with xanomeline alone and none with KarXT (PMID 37036495, n=68). Xanomeline causes atrial bradycardia in vitro (PMID 11181912). Atropine corrects vagal bradycardia (PMID 40645282, PMID 32091590). | **Partly.** The volunteer data suggest trospium removes the syncope signal. | Records check: Cobenfy users on β-blockers or ChEIs, or with conduction disease → syncope, pacemaker, ECG. |
| 2 | **Sialorrhea** (esp. with clozapine) | 4.0 | Anticholinergics treat sialorrhea (PMID 38577753, n=2,378; PMID 37821573). FAERS shows hypersalivation strongly over-reported with Cobenfy (ROR 64.4; PMID 41985280). 25% of clozapine-treated TRS inpatients on Cobenfy developed it (PMID 41694131, n=20). | Only partly; the signal persists. | **Already observed.** This rediscovers a known AE and serves as a positive control for the harm side. |
| 3 | **Hyperhidrosis** | 4.0 | Oxybutynin and BoNT treat it (PMID 34653261, PMID 27976476). In the 52-week KarXT study it occurred in 5.1% (PMID 41506001, n=566). | Partly. | Already observed (positive control). |
| 4 | **Major depression** (central, so trospium cannot protect) | 3.0 | The muscarinic agonist RS-86 induced an anergic-anhedonic syndrome (PMID 2903510). MDD patients are supersensitive to cholinergic REM induction (PMID 7932282, n=119). Scopolamine is antidepressant (PMID 36269510). Scopolamine reversed negative affective bias in rats (PMID 38198569). Counter-evidence: rivastigmine reduced depressive episodes in AD (PMID 22760171). | **No.** The effect is central. | Records check: new depression diagnoses or antidepressant starts after Cobenfy vs. after other antipsychotics. Also check PHQ-9 in schizoaffective/bipolar-depressed patients. |
| 5 | **Dystonia** (incl. antipsychotic-sensitised patients) | 3.0 | ChEI-induced acute dystonia case reports with positive rechallenge (PMID 18029952, PMID 23561545). Xanomeline induced dystonia in EPS-sensitised monkeys (PMID 12700711). BoNT works (PMID 32053883). Counter-evidence: the EMERGENT pooled analysis showed 0.3% dystonia and no scale change (PMID 43010316, n=683). | **No.** The effect is central. | Monitor patients with prior tardive syndromes or dystonia, and PD (links to candidate #1). |

**Runners-up (score 3.0):**
- **Asthma:** glycopyrronium add-on helps (PMID 31582314, n=2,592). Peripheral, so trospium likely protects.
- **Peptic ulcer / GERD:** GERD and dyspepsia ≥5% TEAEs in EMERGENT-2 (PMID 43010223). Bethanechol induces ulcers in rats (PMID 4040471).
- **Epilepsy / myoclonus:** physostigmine increased myoclonic jerks and atropine reduced them (PMID 1620139, n=8). Counter-evidence: donepezil was not linked to seizures (PMID 38580328, n=12,463).

**Cancelled out (score 0):**
- **PD tremor:** a meta-analysis found ChEIs increased tremor (PMID 25224676, n=941). An MDS review found rivastigmine possibly useful for gait (PMID 29570866). The two cancel under the contradiction rule, but the tremor signal is clinically worth monitoring in candidate #1.
- **Myasthenia gravis:** contradictory small studies.
