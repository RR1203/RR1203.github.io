Did recriminalizing drug possession change overdose deaths in Oregon and Washington?

Oregon HB 4002 (1 Sept 2024) and Washington SB 5536 (1 July 2023). Augmented synthetic control on CDC VSRR 12-month-ending overdose deaths, Jan 2019 to Dec 2025. Everything from the overnight run is in this one file: summary, results, figures, novelty search and the full decisions log.

FLAG. The power gate failed: the minimum detectable effect is 25.2%, above the 20% stop threshold. Effect estimates below are exploratory, not evidence. The Washington first stage (arrests) was not obtained. The NFLIS fentanyl-share sensitivity was not run because the site was unreachable. augsynth could not be installed, so ridge-augmented SCM was coded by hand.
  • DoneBuilt state overdose panels for 2015–2025 and an Oregon arrest first stage. Ran augmented SCM with permutation inference for both states, a stacked estimate, robustness checks, and a rerun of the 2021 decriminalization.
  • FoundOregon possession arrests rose 4.7× after HB 4002, so the law was enforced. The design cannot detect effects below about 25%. Exploratory estimates show no detectable effect (OR +4%, p = 0.81; WA +4%, p = 0.77), and the robustness checks swing by ±15–27 points.
  • DecideDrop the project, or re-run the power gate later. A re-run needs final 2026 data (a longer post-period), a registered FBI API key (for Washington arrests) and NFLIS access.

Methods and results (paper-ready draft)

Methods. We used CDC VSRR provisional 12-month-ending drug overdose death counts (predicted values) for 50 states and DC, January 2015 to December 2025, divided by Census population. The policy dates were 1 September 2024 for Oregon (HB 4002) and 1 July 2023 for Washington (SB 5536). Because the outcome is a 12-month rolling sum, the primary estimand was the mean gap over event months 11–15, the first five windows made up entirely of post-policy months. We expressed this gap as a percent of the synthetic control. We estimated ridge-augmented synthetic control weights (simplex-constrained SCM plus a ridge bias correction, with λ chosen on the last 12 pre-period months). Matching used the 2019–pre-policy trajectories of total and synthetic-opioid (T40.4) death rates, each scaled by its SD. The donor pool was the 30 states with complete T40.4 data, excluding OR, WA, CA, CO and NV. We drew inference from in-space permutation (two-sided rank of |% gap|). We estimated power from in-space placebos at four fake pre-period start dates, defining the minimum detectable effect (MDE) as the 95th percentile of |placebo % gap|. The pre-set stop rule was MDE > 20%. Oregon PCS-only arrests came from the Oregon Criminal Justice Commission (LEDS). Analyses used R 4.3.3 with osqp and fixest.

Results. First stage (Oregon): PCS-only arrests averaged 1,044 per quarter before M110 (2018–2020) and 144 under M110 (2021Q2–2024Q2). Under HB 4002 (2024Q4–2025Q4) they averaged 676, which is 4.7 times the M110 level and 0.65 times the pre-M110 level. Power: the MDE was 25.2% (gate failed). It was 28.0% with total-only matching and 20.7% in the Washington design. Exploratory estimates: in Oregon (pre-RMSPE 0.63 per 100k; ASCM weights OK 0.71, AK 0.34), the gap over event months 11–15 was +1.21 per 100k (+4.1%, permutation p = 0.81, RMSPE-ratio p = 0.45, 30 placebos). In the calendar-2025 window it was −2.70 per 100k (−8.6%, about −115 deaths). The robustness checks did not agree with each other. In-time placebos at Sept 2022 and Jan 2023 gave +26.2% and +26.7%. The other specifications gave: total-only +2.9% (p = 0.91); Western donors −14.9%; leave-one-out +7.7% (no AK) and +8.7% (no OK); synthetic-opioid deaths as the outcome −4.2%. The DiD against Western donors gave +18.5% (log coefficient 0.170, 95% CI −0.005 to 0.344, p = 0.055). In Washington, the gap over event months 11–15 was +3.8% (p = 0.77) and over all fully-post windows +16.0% (p = 0.16). Calendar 2024 was +15.1% (about +418 deaths) and 2025 +18.8% (about +469). The robustness checks were: in-time placebo (Jan 2022) +18.1%; total-only +8.9%; Western donors −12.5%; leave-one-out +39.5% (no AK), +2.8% (no ME) and +4.7% (no OK); DiD +48.2% (log 0.393, 95% CI 0.205 to 0.581, p = 0.001). The stacked OR + WA estimate was +3.9% (p = 0.55). Decriminalization replication (Feb 2021, windows Jan–Dec 2022): matched on total + T40.4 with 15 donors, Oregon was +10.6% (p = 0.50) and Washington +5.3% (p = 0.69). Matched on total only with 46 donors, Oregon was +27.2% (p = 0.11) and Washington +3.1%. The implied calendar-2022 excess deaths were 218 for Oregon (403 total-only) and 374 for Washington (334 total-only). The first-year monthly-rate gaps were 0.21 per 100k for Oregon and 0.02 for Washington, against 0.268 and 0.112 in Joshi et al. These estimates fall inside the published range: a fentanyl-adjusted null (Zoorob et al.; Joshi et al.) at one end and sizeable increases (Hall, Hansen & Matsuzawa) at the other. As in that literature, the estimate shrinks when matching accounts for fentanyl.

Verdict

Gate passed (with caveat). A state-level synthetic-control estimate of recriminalization for both OR and WA, with permutation inference and a decriminalization replication in the same setup, appears new. Two working papers touch the recriminalization period and must be read before any write-up.

Decisions log

Run: 2026-11-14, autonomous overnight run (cloud). Every decision that could change a number is logged here, in order.

Environment

  • D01. Network: api.usa.gov, cde.ucr.cjis.gov, nflis.deadiversion.usdoj.gov, www.oregon.gov were blocked; added to the environment allowlist. nflis.deadiversion.usdoj.gov still fails (proxy 502, upstream unreachable) → NFLIS fentanyl-share sensitivity not run (see D14).
  • D02. R 4.3.3 installed from Ubuntu apt; packages from CRAN source. augsynth is GitHub-only and GitHub is blocked → augmented SCM (ridge ASCM, Ben-Michael, Feller & Rothstein, JASA 2021) implemented directly in the Rmd with osqp for the simplex-constrained SCM weights plus the closed-form ridge bias correction. This is a deviation from house style (no helper functions): one estimator function is defined in 00. SETUP because the same estimator runs > 1,000 times (placebos). All models are still called explicitly, one line per specification.

Step 0 gates

  • D03. Novelty: no published estimate of the recriminalization effect found (see 00_novelty.md). Two 2026 working papers (Pourtaheri; Jones et al.) mention the recriminalization period; full texts could not be read. Gate passed with caveat.
  • D04. First stage, Oregon: FBI CDE API with DEMO_KEY is limited to 10 requests/hour and shared; offense codes are undocumented in the API (codes 185 and 35A return all zeros). Primary Oregon first stage therefore uses the Oregon Criminal Justice Commission PCS-only arrest series (LEDS, quarterly 2018Q1–2025Q4), extracted from the plotly data embedded in CJC, Examining Equity in the Recriminalization of Drug Possession in Oregon (Feb 2026, oregon.gov). PCS-only = arrests whose only charge is possession; excludes possession arrests with other charges. The 2024Q3 value mixes 2 pre- and 1 post-month.
  • D05. First stage, Washington and FBI cross-check: FBI CDE arrest endpoint, offense code 18 (UCR "drug abuse violations", possession + sale combined; possession-only subcode not identified), monthly. A registered API key would remove the rate limit (api.data.gov signup needs an e-mail; not done in an unattended run).
  • D06. Outcome data: CDC VSRR Provisional Drug Overdose Death Counts (data.cdc.gov xkb8-kh2a), downloaded 2026-11-14. Latest month = June 2026 (12-month ending). All states' 2026 rows carry the footnote "Underreported due to incomplete data", and Oregon's 2026 counts are far below predicted (e.g., Feb 2026 reported 932 vs predicted 1,084). → Analysis window ends December 2025 (last month without the underreporting flag in any state).
  • D07. Outcome version: (a) 12-month-ending series used as a smoothed outcome. Monthly counts cannot be recovered from 12-month-ending sums without 12 anchor months, and CDC WONDER's API refuses state-level grouping (web form only). Consequence: an effect starting at month 0 appears gradually; the 12-month window ending at event month 11 is the first window made entirely of post-policy months.
  • D08. Use predicted values (CDC adjustment for pending investigations) rather than reported; they differ by < 1% for OR/WA before 2026. Rates = predicted 12-month count / Census July-1 population of the window's end year × 100,000 (Vintage 2025 for 2020–2025; Vintage 2020 for 2019; 2015–2018 from Vintage 2020 file for the decriminalization replication).

Design

  • D09. Primary estimand (Oregon): mean gap between Oregon and synthetic Oregon in the 12-month-ending overdose death rate over fully-post windows (event months 11–15 = Aug–Dec 2025), expressed as % of synthetic Oregon. Secondary: gap in the window ending Dec 2025 (calendar 2025), and the full monthly path.
  • D10. Washington: event 0 = July 2023. Same estimand at event months 11–15 (June–Oct 2024) for alignment with Oregon; also mean over all fully-post windows (event 11–29, June 2024–Dec 2025).
  • D11. Pre-period: Jan 2019 – month before policy (OR: Aug 2024, 68 months; WA: June 2023, 54 months). Note: OR and WA pre-periods contain their own 2021 decriminalization; the SCM only matches the trajectory.
  • D12. Donor pool: 50 states + DC minus OR, WA, and states with a known change to hard-drug possession law 2019–2025: California (Prop 36, effective Dec 2024, felony for repeat possession), Colorado (HB19-1263 reclassified possession to misdemeanor, Mar 2020; HB22-1326 fentanyl possession felony, Jul 2022). Nevada (AB236, possession thresholds, Jul 2020) also excluded as a precaution. [REF NEEDED: legal citations for these three; no full 50-state legal review was done — any other state change would leave a contaminated donor.] Puerto Rico, NYC ("YC") and US total excluded.
  • D13. Fentanyl timing: SCM matches on both the pre-period trajectory of the total overdose rate and the synthetic-opioid (T40.4) death rate, each block scaled by its pre-period SD across units. Donors with any missing T40.4 value in the analysis window are dropped from the main donor pool (count logged in the Rmd); a sensitivity matches on total deaths only with these donors added back.
  • D14. NFLIS fentanyl share: public NFLIS site unreachable → not run. Logged as a deviation.
  • D15. Ridge penalty λ for ASCM: chosen by holdout validation — fit on pre-period minus the last 12 pre months, choose λ from a fixed grid minimizing RMSE on those 12 months, then refit on full pre-period. SCM weights have a tiny ridge (1e-6) for numerical stability.
  • D16. Inference: in-space placebos — each donor in turn treated as Oregon, with the remaining donors as its pool, same estimand. Two-sided permutation p = rank of |Oregon| among |all| (Oregon included). Also the classic post/pre RMSPE ratio p-value.
  • D17. Power: in-space placebo distribution at fake start dates inside the pre-period (Sept 2021, Jan 2022, Sept 2022, Jan 2023; each with 16 post months that end before the true date). Minimum detectable effect (MDE) = the 95th percentile of |placebo % effect| pooled across states and fake dates (i.e., the smallest effect that would rank in the top 5%). Stop rule: MDE > 20%.
  • D18. In-time placebos (Oregon): fake starts Sept 2022 and Jan 2023 ("2023"; Sept 2023 would leave fewer than 16 months before the true date).
  • D19. Stacked OR + WA: average of the two % effects at aligned event months 11–15. Placebo distribution: for each donor state, the average of its placebo % effect in the OR design and in the WA design.
  • D20. DiD cross-check: TWFE on log 12-month-ending rate, treated × fully-post, state and month FE, Western donors only, partially-post windows (event 0–10) dropped; SE clustered by state (few clusters → treat SE as indicative).
  • D21. Decriminalization replication: OR (M110, Feb 2021) and WA (Blake, Feb 2021 — Joshi et al. used Mar 2021) with event 0 = Feb 2021, pre-period Jan 2015–Jan 2021, same donor rules; fully-post windows Jan–Dec 2022 (event 11–22). Compared with published ranges, and converted to excess deaths for calendar 2022 and 2023 windows.

Added during the run

  • D22. Ridge λ grid extended from 10^-3…10^2 to 10^-5…10^2 after the first knit showed λ at the lower edge for both states. After the change Oregon still picks the edge (λ = 1e-4 relative), Washington picks 0.01. Near-zero λ means the ridge correction is almost unpenalized (weights may be negative; ASCM weights in Oregon: OK 0.71, AK 0.34).
  • D23. Donor pool after the T40.4 completeness rule: 30 states (16 dropped for missing synthetic-opioid values: AL AR FL ID IN KS LA MI MN MS MT NC ND NE PA WY). Decriminalization replication (2015–2022 window): only 15 donors have complete T40.4 data; the total-only version uses 46.
  • D24. Power gate FAILED. MDE (95th pct of |placebo % effect|, 4 fake dates × 31 states) = 25.2% > 20% threshold. Gate sensitivity: total-only matching 28.0%; WA design 20.7%. Per the protocol the run should STOP here.
